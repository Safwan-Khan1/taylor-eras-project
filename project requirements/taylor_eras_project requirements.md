12\. Taylor Swift Eras: Multimodal Classification of Artistic Periods Background: An artist\'s discography naturally segments into distinct stylistic periods. Quantifying this evolution requires analyzing both the semantic weight of the lyrics and the digital signal properties of the audio. Overview: A stylometric and acoustic analysis project aiming to classify unknown tracks into highly specific sub-categories, demonstrating the superiority of multimodal architectures over unimodal baselines. Objective: Evaluate the efficacy of late-fusion versus early-fusion architectures when combining dense numerical audio vectors with sparse, high-dimensional textual vectors. Dataset: Lyrical corpora extracted via the Genius API, matched explicitly to digital acoustic signatures pulled from the Spotify Web API. Main Components: Word2Vec or TF-IDF for lyrical vectorization, Scikit-Learn pipelines for audio feature scaling, and Stacking Classifiers combining Support Vector Machines (SVM) with Random Forests. Deliverables: An interactive web portal allowing users to analyze any arbitrary pop song, concluding with a confusion matrix analysis highlighting which \"Eras\" the model struggles to differentiate. Bonus/Extra Components: Utilize topic modeling techniques like Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) on the lyrics to mathematically prove semantic shifts in vocabulary across different stages of the artist\'s career.
